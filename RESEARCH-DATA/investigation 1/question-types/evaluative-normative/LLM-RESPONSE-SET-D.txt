A1d) Government funding should strike a balance between pure and applied mathematics. Pure mathematics drives theoretical innovation and long-term breakthroughs, while applied mathematics addresses immediate societal and technological needs. Prioritizing one exclusively may hinder either foundational knowledge or practical problem-solving.

A2d) Enforcing standardized math tests can be ethically defensible if they are used to identify learning gaps and improve education. However, over-reliance on testing may cause stress and limit creative learning, so ethical implementation must ensure fairness, accessibility, and support for diverse learning styles.

A3d) Requiring all university students to complete advanced mathematics is generally not justified. While mathematical literacy is valuable, mandating advanced courses for unrelated majors may be inefficient and burdensome. Focused, elective math courses aligned with student goals are more appropriate.

A4d) Physicists should give priority to renewable energy research due to its immediate impact on climate change and global sustainability. Fundamental particle physics is important, but renewable energy research addresses urgent societal needs and can provide tangible benefits in the short term.

A5d) It is not justifiable to prioritize space exploration over climate change programs. While space research has long-term scientific value, addressing climate change directly affects human survival and environmental sustainability, making it an ethically higher priority.

A6d) Governments should limit nuclear research cautiously. Potential dangers like accidents or proliferation justify regulation, but complete restriction could hinder energy innovation. Ethical governance requires balancing safety with scientific progress under strict oversight.

A7d) Chemical companies should be legally required to adopt green practices. Environmental harm and public health risks outweigh the costs of implementation, making regulation necessary to protect society and ecosystems while encouraging sustainable innovation.

A8d) Animal testing is ethically questionable if alternatives exist. High costs alone do not justify harm. Research should prioritize non-animal methods, and animal testing should only be a last resort under strict ethical review.

A9d) Governments should subsidize new drug development. High initial costs hinder innovation, and public support ensures that essential medicines reach society, promoting health equity and addressing urgent medical needs.

A10d) Genetic modification of crops can be promoted to address hunger if ecological risks are carefully managed. The ethical priority of reducing human suffering justifies cautious adoption, combined with strict environmental monitoring.

A11d) Biological research on humans with informed consent is ethically defensible. Respecting autonomy and ensuring voluntary participation protects individuals while allowing valuable scientific progress.

A12d) Governments should balance funding between biomedical and ecological research. Biomedical research addresses health crises, but ecological studies ensure long-term sustainability, making both ethically important.

A13d) AI development should be strictly controlled to prevent societal harm. Unchecked AI can lead to inequality, privacy violations, and safety risks, so ethical oversight is necessary while encouraging beneficial innovation.

A14d) Collecting user data for profit with consent is morally acceptable if privacy is respected and users are fully informed. Ethical concerns arise if consent is coerced or misleading, highlighting the importance of transparency.

A15d) Digital literacy should be required in schools. It equips students to navigate modern society, reduces inequality, and supports safe, informed use of technology, making it an essential skill alongside traditional subjects.

A16d) Historical monuments of controversial figures should be contextualized rather than removed. Maintaining them with explanatory information promotes education, critical thinking, and awareness of historical complexity.

A17d) Modifying history textbooks to reflect modern ethical standards is defensible if done transparently. It ensures education aligns with current moral understanding while retaining historical accuracy and multiple perspectives.

A18d) Governments should fund museums that showcase multiple historical perspectives. Encouraging diverse narratives fosters critical thinking, empathy, and a more nuanced understanding of history.

A19d) Philosophical teachings that conflict with religion should be included in curricula. Exposure to diverse ideas develops critical thinking, ethical reasoning, and respect for different worldviews without imposing beliefs.

A20d) Preferring utilitarian strategies in public policy can be ethically permissible when decisions maximize overall well-being. However, deontological principles should guide actions to avoid unjust harm to individuals, balancing both approaches.

A21d) Moral instruction should be mandatory in schools. Teaching ethical reasoning helps students make informed decisions, develop empathy, and navigate complex social situations, supporting societal cohesion.

A22d) Universal basic income can be justified to address economic disparity. It provides financial security, reduces poverty, and promotes social equity, though careful implementation is needed to maintain economic stability.

A23d) Imposing tariffs to protect local industries can be ethically defensible if it safeguards livelihoods and economic stability. However, negative impacts on global trade and foreign economies require careful consideration and balance.

A24d) Raising wealth taxes to support social welfare is ethically justified. It promotes redistribution, reduces inequality, and funds essential public services, balancing societal needs with economic incentives.

A25d) Teaching advanced grammar should not take precedence over creative writing. While technical skills are important, fostering creativity, expression, and critical thinking is equally vital for holistic language education.

A26d) Enforcing strict public language use to maintain cultural identity is ethically questionable. Respecting linguistic diversity and individual expression should be prioritized while promoting cultural heritage through education and community engagement.

A27d) Controversial literature should not be censored in schools. Exposure to challenging ideas encourages critical thinking, dialogue, and understanding, while guided discussion mitigates potential harm.

A28d) Ethical standards should be imposed on new medical technologies even if innovation slows. Protecting patient safety and societal trust is paramount, ensuring progress does not compromise well-being.

A29d) Distributing limited medical resources based on social benefit can be ethically acceptable in emergencies to maximize overall welfare. However, fairness and equity must guide decisions to avoid discrimination or neglect of vulnerable individuals.

A30d) Mandatory organ donation is ethically contentious. While it could save lives, it infringes on individual autonomy. A preferable approach is incentivized or opt-out systems that respect choice while encouraging donation.
