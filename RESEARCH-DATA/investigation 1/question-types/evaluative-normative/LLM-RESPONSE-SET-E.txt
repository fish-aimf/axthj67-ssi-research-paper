A1e) Funding should balance pure and applied mathematics. Pure mathematics drives fundamental understanding and long-term innovation, while applied mathematics addresses immediate societal problems. Prioritizing one exclusively may hinder overall scientific progress.

A2e) Mandating standardized math tests is ethically defensible if used to ensure basic competency and equal opportunities, but it becomes problematic if it narrows curriculum or unfairly penalizes students from disadvantaged backgrounds.

A3e) Universities should not require all students to take advanced mathematics. While mathematical literacy is valuable, forcing advanced courses on unrelated fields can waste time and create unnecessary stress.

A4e) Research in renewable energy should take precedence due to urgent climate concerns and immediate societal benefit, though fundamental particle physics remains important for long-term scientific advancement.

A5e) Allocating more funds to climate change mitigation is justifiable because it addresses immediate existential threats, whereas space exploration, though valuable, has less immediate impact on human survival.

A6e) Governments should restrict nuclear research only to high-risk applications. Responsible regulation can prevent catastrophic risks without stifling scientific development in safer areas.

A7e) Chemical companies should be legally required to adopt green methods when feasible. However, mandates should consider technological and economic practicality to avoid crippling innovation.

A8e) Animal testing is ethically questionable if alternatives exist, even if expensive. Ethical practice should prioritize reducing harm to sentient beings whenever possible.

A9e) Subsidizing new pharmaceuticals is justifiable because high costs hinder innovation that can save lives. Public support ensures essential medicines reach the market.

A10e) Promoting genetically modified crops is defensible to combat hunger, provided ecological risks are monitored and managed responsibly.

A11e) Biological experiments on humans with informed consent are ethically defensible, as consent respects autonomy and participants are aware of risks.

A12e) Funding biomedical research should be prioritized due to direct human health impact, but ecological studies remain crucial for long-term planetary sustainability.

A13e) AI development should be strictly regulated to prevent societal harm, ensuring ethical deployment, safety, and fairness while fostering innovation responsibly.

A14e) Collecting user data for profit is morally acceptable if fully transparent and consensual, but privacy protections must be enforced.

A15e) Digital literacy should be compulsory to equip students with essential skills for modern life, employment, and informed participation in society.

A16e) Monuments linked to controversial figures should be preserved with context, educating the public rather than erasing history.

A17e) Revising textbooks to reflect contemporary ethics is defensible if it promotes critical thinking, but it must not distort historical facts.

A18e) Governments should fund museums presenting multiple perspectives to foster understanding, tolerance, and informed citizenship.

A19e) Schools may teach philosophical concepts that conflict with religious beliefs to promote critical thinking, provided they respect students’ freedom of belief.

A20e) Favoring utilitarian policies is defensible when maximizing overall well-being, but must be balanced with deontological principles to protect rights.

A21e) Moral education should be mandatory to develop ethical reasoning and empathy, preparing students to navigate complex societal challenges.

A22e) Universal basic income is justifiable to reduce inequality and provide security, especially in societies facing automation and economic instability.

A23e) Imposing tariffs to protect local industries can be justified to safeguard employment and domestic growth, but potential global trade consequences must be considered.

A24e) Raising wealth taxes is defensible to fund social welfare, promote equity, and reduce disparities in access to essential resources.

A25e) Schools should balance advanced grammar and creative writing; prioritizing one over the other limits either technical proficiency or expressive development.

A26e) Enforcing language standards publicly is defensible to preserve cultural identity, but should allow individual expression and linguistic evolution.

A27e) Controversial literature should not be censored; exposure promotes critical thinking, discussion, and understanding of diverse perspectives.

A28e) Ethical regulations on emerging medical technologies are necessary, even if innovation slows, to prevent harm and maintain public trust.

A29e) Allocating scarce medical resources based on social utility is ethically controversial; prioritizing need ensures fairness, while utility maximizes overall benefit. Balance is essential.

A30e) Mandatory organ donation is ethically contentious; while it saves lives, it challenges individual autonomy. Voluntary opt-out systems may be a balanced approach.
