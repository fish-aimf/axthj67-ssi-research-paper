A1b) Governments should balance funding between pure and applied mathematics. Pure mathematics drives theoretical breakthroughs, while applied mathematics solves real-world problems. Prioritizing one over the other could hinder either innovation or practical solutions, so funding should reflect both long-term and immediate societal benefits.

A2b) Standardized math tests can be ethically defensible if they are fair, accessible, and used to improve learning rather than punish students. They provide objective measures of skills, but overreliance can harm creativity and widen inequities, so ethical implementation requires careful design and contextual interpretation.

A3b) Universities should not mandate advanced mathematics for all students. While basic numeracy is important, forcing advanced courses on unrelated majors may waste resources and cause unnecessary stress. Tailored math requirements based on field relevance are more reasonable.

A4b) Physicists should prioritize renewable energy research due to urgent climate needs. While fundamental particle physics expands knowledge, renewable energy research has immediate societal impact by mitigating climate change and supporting sustainable development.

A5b) Funding climate change initiatives is more justifiable than space exploration because immediate environmental threats affect billions. Space exploration offers long-term benefits, but addressing current planetary crises should take precedence for ethical and practical reasons.

A6b) Governments should regulate nuclear research to mitigate risks while allowing scientific advancement under strict safety protocols. Completely restricting research may prevent innovation, but safety must remain a priority to protect society.

A7b) Chemical firms should be mandated to adopt eco-friendly production if feasible. Environmental protection benefits society broadly, and legal requirements ensure compliance, encouraging sustainable industry practices while balancing economic viability.

A8b) Using animals in chemical testing is ethically acceptable only when alternatives are genuinely unavailable and tests are conducted humanely. Cost alone does not justify unnecessary harm; ethical responsibility requires minimizing animal suffering wherever possible.

A9b) Governments should subsidize new drug development despite high costs, as initial investment can lead to life-saving treatments, public health improvements, and long-term economic benefits through innovation.

A10b) Promoting genetically modified crops is justifiable if it alleviates hunger effectively, but ecological risks must be managed carefully. The ethical priority of saving lives can outweigh potential environmental concerns with responsible regulation.

A11b) Human subject research with informed consent is ethically acceptable. Consent respects autonomy and allows participants to make informed decisions, provided research minimizes harm and upholds ethical standards.

A12b) Funding should balance biomedical and ecological research. Biomedical research directly improves human health, while ecological studies ensure long-term planetary sustainability. Prioritization should reflect both immediate and future societal needs.

A13b) AI research should be strictly regulated to prevent societal risks. Strong oversight ensures ethical development, prevents misuse, and protects privacy, while still allowing beneficial technological progress.

A14b) It is morally acceptable for software companies to profit from user data if consent is informed and freely given. Ethical concerns arise when consent is misleading, coerced, or users lack understanding, so transparency is key.

A15b) Digital literacy education should be compulsory, as it equips students with essential skills to navigate, evaluate, and responsibly use technology in modern society, promoting informed citizenship and employability.

A16b) Monuments linked to controversial figures should be preserved with contextual information rather than removed. This approach educates about history without glorifying harmful actions, promoting critical understanding.

A17b) Revising history textbooks to reflect current ethical standards is defensible if done transparently. It helps students understand evolving social norms, but historical accuracy and multiple perspectives should remain central.

A18b) Governments should support museums presenting multiple historical perspectives. Such institutions encourage critical thinking, empathy, and understanding of complex histories, fostering informed and tolerant citizens.

A19b) Schools may teach philosophies conflicting with religious beliefs to promote critical thinking and awareness of diverse viewpoints. Education should respect freedom of thought while avoiding indoctrination.

A20b) Prioritizing utilitarianism over deontology can be ethically permissible in public policy when maximizing societal benefit outweighs strict rule adherence. However, moral rules and rights should not be ignored entirely to prevent injustice.

A21b) Moral education should be mandatory, as it helps students develop ethical reasoning, empathy, and social responsibility, which are essential for personal development and societal cohesion.

A22b) Universal basic income is justifiable as a tool to combat economic inequality, providing financial security, reducing poverty, and supporting social stability, though it should complement, not replace, targeted welfare programs.

A23b) Imposing tariffs to protect domestic industries can be justified to safeguard jobs and economic stability, but policymakers must consider global trade impacts and avoid harming international relations unnecessarily.

A24b) Raising wealth taxes to support social welfare is defensible, as it redistributes resources to reduce inequality and fund essential public services, promoting societal fairness while balancing economic incentives.

A25b) Schools should prioritize creative writing over advanced grammar. While grammar is important, fostering creativity, expression, and critical thinking prepares students for diverse communication challenges.

A26b) Strict public language standards are not fully justifiable solely to maintain cultural identity. Encouraging language preservation is positive, but rigid enforcement can suppress diversity and individual expression.

A27b) Controversial books should not be restricted in education. Exposure to diverse ideas promotes critical thinking, debate, and intellectual growth, though guidance and contextual discussion are important.

A28b) Ethical regulations on new medical technologies are justifiable even if innovation slows. Protecting human welfare, safety, and rights outweighs the benefits of rapid technological advancement.

A29b) Distributing limited medical resources based on social utility is ethically contentious. Prioritizing those with the greatest societal impact can be justified in crises, but fairness and need must also guide allocation.

A30b) Mandatory organ donation is ethically debatable. While it could save lives, it conflicts with bodily autonomy. Incentives or opt-out systems may balance public health benefits with individual rights.
