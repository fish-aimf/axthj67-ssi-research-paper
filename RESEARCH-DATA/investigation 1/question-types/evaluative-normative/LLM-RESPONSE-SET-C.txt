A1c) Governments should balance funding between pure and applied mathematics. Pure mathematics drives theoretical breakthroughs and long-term innovation, while applied mathematics addresses immediate societal needs. Favoring one exclusively risks neglecting the other.

A2c) Mandating standardized math testing can be ethically permissible if it ensures fairness and objective assessment, but it must account for diverse learning styles and avoid harming students’ self-esteem or reinforcing inequities.

A3c) Universities should not require advanced mathematics for all students. While numeracy is valuable, imposing advanced math on students whose fields do not require it can be unnecessarily burdensome and may detract from specialized learning.

A4c) Physicists should balance renewable energy research with fundamental particle physics. While renewable energy has immediate societal impact, fundamental research drives long-term scientific understanding and technological innovation.

A5c) Funding climate change mitigation should take priority over space programs. Addressing immediate environmental threats ensures survival and sustainability, whereas space exploration, though valuable, is less urgent.

A6c) Governments should regulate nuclear research due to significant risks. Safety and public welfare outweigh unrestricted scientific progress, but regulations should allow controlled research to continue advancing knowledge.

A7c) Chemical companies should be legally obligated to implement sustainable production methods. Environmental responsibility is a societal duty, and legal enforcement ensures industry-wide adherence and protection of public health.

A8c) Animal testing should be minimized, especially if cheaper alternatives exist. Ethical principles favor reducing harm when possible, even if alternatives are costly, to protect animal welfare.

A9c) Governments should support pharmaceutical development despite high costs. Investing in new drugs can save lives, advance medical knowledge, and provide long-term societal benefits.

A10c) Genetically modified crops can be promoted cautiously to reduce hunger. Benefits must be weighed against ecological risks, and strict regulation should ensure environmental safety.

A11c) Conducting biological experiments on humans with informed consent is ethically defensible. Respecting autonomy and ensuring full disclosure protects participants while advancing scientific knowledge.

A12c) Funding should balance biomedical research with ecological studies. Both are crucial for human health and environmental sustainability; prioritizing one exclusively may create long-term societal risks.

A13c) AI research should be heavily regulated to prevent harm. Ensuring safety, privacy, and ethical use protects society while allowing innovation under controlled conditions.

A14c) Collecting and monetizing user data with consent is ethically acceptable. Transparency and informed consent allow users to make autonomous choices about their personal information.

A15c) Digital literacy should be mandatory. Equipping students with essential technology skills promotes equal opportunities, responsible online behavior, and prepares them for modern workplaces.

A16c) Controversial monuments should be contextualized rather than automatically dismantled. Preserving history while providing educational context fosters critical thinking without glorifying harmful actions.

A17c) Updating history textbooks to align with contemporary ethical norms is defensible. It helps students understand historical events through modern perspectives, promoting ethical reflection and awareness.

A18c) Governments should fund museums presenting diverse viewpoints. Inclusive historical representation encourages critical thinking, cultural understanding, and social cohesion.

A19c) Schools should teach philosophical ideas that contradict religion. Exposure to diverse perspectives develops critical thinking, tolerance, and intellectual growth while respecting freedom of belief.

A20c) Preferring utilitarian policies over deontological ones can be ethically defensible in governance. Maximizing overall societal welfare may outweigh rigid rule-based approaches in practical decision-making.

A21c) Moral education should be compulsory. Teaching ethics promotes empathy, social responsibility, and informed decision-making among students, fostering a morally aware society.

A22c) Universal basic income is justifiable to reduce inequality. Providing financial security enhances social welfare, reduces poverty, and empowers individuals to pursue education and employment opportunities.

A23c) Imposing tariffs to protect local industries is defensible but should be limited. Protecting domestic jobs can be beneficial, but excessive protectionism may harm global trade and long-term economic growth.

A24c) Raising wealth taxes to fund social welfare is ethically defensible. Redistribution supports vulnerable populations, reduces inequality, and promotes social justice.

A25c) Schools should balance advanced grammar and creative writing. Over-prioritizing grammar can stifle creativity, while developing writing skills fosters communication and critical thinking.

A26c) Enforcing strict language standards publicly is defensible for cultural preservation but should allow flexibility. Protecting linguistic heritage is important, yet rigid enforcement can suppress individual expression.

A27c) Restricting controversial literature in schools is not justified. Exposure to diverse ideas encourages critical thinking and dialogue, while guidance can help contextualize sensitive content.

A28c) Ethical rules should govern emerging medical technologies, even if development slows. Prioritizing safety, human rights, and long-term consequences outweighs speed of innovation.

A29c) Allocating scarce medical resources based on social utility is defensible in extreme cases. Prioritizing treatments that save the most lives or societal function can be ethically justified, though equity concerns must be considered.
