A1b) Conduct a randomized controlled study with two groups of high school students: one taught using traditional lecture methods, the other using interactive problem-solving sessions. Independent variable: teaching method. Dependent variable: student performance on standardized calculus tests. Controls: same teacher experience, class size, curriculum, and testing conditions. Evaluation: pre- and post-tests, normalized gain scores, and statistical comparison using t-tests or ANOVA.

A2b) Use a plane mirror and a protractor to measure angles of incidence and reflection. Equipment: laser pointer, mirror, protractor, ruler, screen. Procedure: shine the laser at various angles onto the mirror, record reflected angle for each. Independent variable: angle of incidence. Dependent variable: angle of reflection. Controls: same mirror, laser, and room conditions. Analysis: plot angle of incidence vs angle of reflection and perform linear regression to verify the law of reflection.

A3b) React a fixed concentration of reactant at different temperatures (e.g., 20°C, 30°C, 40°C) and measure product formation over time using spectrophotometry. Independent variable: temperature. Dependent variable: reaction rate. Controls: same reactant concentration, solvent, volume, and apparatus. Measurement: time-based absorbance readings to calculate rate constants. Analysis: Arrhenius plot to determine activation energy.
