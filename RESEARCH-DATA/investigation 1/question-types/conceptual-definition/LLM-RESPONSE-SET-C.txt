A1c) A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself.  
A2c) In mathematics, a function is a relation that assigns exactly one output to each input from a given set.  
A3c) In calculus, a derivative represents the rate at which a function's value changes with respect to a change in its input.  

A4c) Newton's first law of motion states that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force.  
A5c) Electric current is the flow of electric charge, usually measured in amperes (A).  
A6c) Gravitational potential energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its position in a gravitational field, usually relative to the Earth.  

A7c) According to Brønsted-Lowry, an acid is a substance that can donate a proton (H⁺) to another substance.  
A8c) A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed in the process.  
A9c) Oxidation is a chemical process in which a substance loses electrons, often involving the addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen.  

A10c) Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants use sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.  
A11c) Natural selection is the process by which organisms better adapted to their environment tend to survive and produce more offspring.  
A12c) DNA replication is the biological process of producing two identical copies of DNA from one original DNA molecule.  

A13c) A computer algorithm is a step-by-step procedure or set of rules for solving a problem or performing a task.  
A14c) Object-oriented programming is a programming paradigm based on the concept of objects, which contain data and methods.  
A15c) A database is an organized collection of structured information or data, typically stored and accessed electronically.  

A16c) Feudalism was a social, economic, and political system in medieval Europe where land was exchanged for military service and labor.  
A17c) The Renaissance was a period of cultural, artistic, and intellectual revival in Europe, roughly from the 14th to 17th century.  
A18c) The Industrial Revolution was the transition to new manufacturing processes in the 18th and 19th centuries, including mechanization and mass production.  

A19c) Utilitarianism is a philosophical theory that holds that the best action is the one that maximizes overall happiness or well-being.  
A20c) Epistemology is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature, sources, and limits of knowledge.  
A21c) Existentialism is a philosophical movement emphasizing individual freedom, choice, and the search for meaning in an often meaningless world.  

A22c) Supply and demand is an economic model where the price and quantity of goods are determined by the availability of supply and the desire for them (demand).  
A23c) Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, reducing purchasing power.  
A24c) Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that must be forgone when a choice is made.  

A25c) A metaphor is a figure of speech that describes an object or action by referring to something else, implying a symbolic similarity.  
A26c) Syntax in grammar refers to the set of rules that govern the structure of sentences in a language.  
A27c) Narrative point of view is the perspective from which a story is told, such as first person, second person, or third person.  

A28c) Informed consent is the process by which a person voluntarily agrees to a procedure or action after understanding its risks, benefits, and alternatives.  
A29c) The principle of beneficence in ethics refers to the obligation to act in ways that promote the well-being of others.  
A30c) Moral relativism is the view that moral judgments and ethical standards are not absolute but vary according to culture, context, or individual perspective.
