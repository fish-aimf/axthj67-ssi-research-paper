A1d) A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself.  
A2d) In mathematics, a function is a relation that assigns exactly one output to each input from a given set.  
A3d) A derivative in calculus represents the rate at which a function changes with respect to a variable; it is the slope of the tangent line at a point on a curve.  

A4d) Newton's first law of motion states that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.  
A5d) Electric current is the flow of electric charge, typically measured in amperes (A).  
A6d) Gravitational potential energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its position in a gravitational field, typically relative to the Earth's surface.  

A7d) An acid, according to the Brønsted-Lowry definition, is a substance that donates a proton (H⁺ ion) to another substance.  
A8d) A chemical catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed in the process.  
A9d) Oxidation is the loss of electrons by a substance during a chemical reaction, often associated with gaining oxygen or losing hydrogen.  

A10d) Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy, producing glucose and oxygen from carbon dioxide and water.  
A11d) Natural selection is the process by which organisms better adapted to their environment tend to survive and produce more offspring.  
A12d) DNA replication is the biological process of producing two identical copies of DNA from one original DNA molecule.  

A13d) A computer algorithm is a step-by-step procedure or set of rules for solving a specific problem or performing a computation.  
A14d) Object-oriented programming is a programming paradigm that uses objects, which contain data and methods, to design and organize software.  
A15d) A database is an organized collection of data that can be easily accessed, managed, and updated.  

A16d) Feudalism is a historical social and economic system in which land was held by lords in exchange for military service, and peasants worked the land for the lord.  
A17d) The Renaissance refers to the cultural, artistic, and intellectual revival in Europe between the 14th and 17th centuries, marking the transition from the Middle Ages to modernity.  
A18d) The Industrial Revolution was a period of major industrialization during the 18th and 19th centuries characterized by the development of machinery, factories, and mass production.  

A19d) Utilitarianism is a philosophical theory that advocates actions that maximize overall happiness or well-being for the greatest number of people.  
A20d) Epistemology is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature, origin, and limits of knowledge.  
A21d) Existentialism is a philosophical movement that emphasizes individual freedom, choice, and responsibility, often focusing on the meaning of human existence.  

A22d) Supply and demand is an economic model that describes how the price and quantity of goods are determined by the availability of supply and the desire of consumers.  
A23d) Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, reducing purchasing power.  
A24d) Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative foregone when making a choice.  

A25d) A metaphor is a figure of speech in which a word or phrase is applied to an object or action to which it is not literally applicable, implying a symbolic resemblance.  
A26d) Syntax refers to the set of rules that governs the structure and arrangement of words in sentences.  
A27d) Narrative point of view is the perspective from which a story is told, such as first person, second person, or third person.  

A28d) Informed consent is the ethical process by which a person voluntarily agrees to participate in an activity or treatment after being informed of all relevant facts, risks, and benefits.  
A29d) Beneficence is the ethical principle of acting in ways that promote the well-being and welfare of others.  
A30d) Moral relativism is the philosophical view that moral judgments and ethical standards are not absolute but are shaped by cultural, societal, or individual perspectives.
