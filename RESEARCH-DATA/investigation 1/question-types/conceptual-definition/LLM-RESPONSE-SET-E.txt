A1e) A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself.  
A2e) A mathematical function is a relation that assigns exactly one output value to each input value from a given set.  
A3e) A derivative in calculus represents the rate at which a function changes with respect to its variable; it is the slope of the tangent line at a point on the function.  

A4e) Newton's first law states that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force.  
A5e) Electric current is the flow of electric charge, typically measured in amperes, through a conductor.  
A6e) Gravitational potential energy is the energy an object possesses due to its position in a gravitational field, usually relative to the Earth's surface.  

A7e) According to Brønsted-Lowry, an acid is a substance that can donate a proton (H⁺ ion) to another substance.  
A8e) A catalyst is a substance that speeds up a chemical reaction without being consumed or permanently altered.  
A9e) Oxidation is the process in which an atom, ion, or molecule loses electrons, often associated with gaining oxygen or losing hydrogen.  

A10e) Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and some other organisms use sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water to produce glucose and oxygen.  
A11e) Natural selection is the process by which organisms better adapted to their environment tend to survive and produce more offspring.  
A12e) DNA replication is the process by which a cell makes an identical copy of its DNA before cell division.  

A13e) A computer algorithm is a step-by-step set of instructions designed to perform a specific task or solve a problem.  
A14e) Object-oriented programming is a programming paradigm that organizes data and behavior into objects, which can interact with one another.  
A15e) A database is an organized collection of structured information or data that can be easily accessed, managed, and updated.  

A16e) Feudalism is a social and economic system in medieval Europe in which land was held in exchange for service or labor.  
A17e) The Renaissance was a cultural, artistic, and intellectual revival in Europe between the 14th and 17th centuries, emphasizing humanism and classical learning.  
A18e) The Industrial Revolution refers to the period of rapid industrial growth and technological innovation that began in the late 18th century, transforming economies and societies.  

A19e) Utilitarianism is an ethical theory that suggests the best action is the one that maximizes overall happiness or well-being.  
A20e) Epistemology is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature, origin, and scope of knowledge.  
A21e) Existentialism is a philosophical movement emphasizing individual freedom, choice, and responsibility in creating meaning in life.  

A22e) Supply and demand is an economic model describing how the price and quantity of goods are determined by the relationship between availability (supply) and desire (demand).  
A23e) Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, reducing purchasing power.  
A24e) Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that must be forgone when a choice is made.  

A25e) A metaphor is a figure of speech in which a word or phrase is applied to an object or action to which it is not literally applicable, suggesting a comparison.  
A26e) Syntax is the set of rules that governs the structure of sentences and the arrangement of words in a language.  
A27e) Narrative point of view refers to the perspective from which a story is told, such as first person, second person, or third person.  

A28e) Informed consent is the process by which a person voluntarily agrees to a proposed action or treatment after being informed of its risks, benefits, and alternatives.  
A29e) Beneficence is the ethical principle of promoting good and acting in ways that benefit others.  
A30e) Moral relativism is the philosophical view that moral judgments and ethical standards are not absolute but vary based on culture, context, or individual perspective.
