A1a) A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself.  

A2a) A function in mathematics is a relation that assigns each input exactly one output.  

A3a) In calculus, a derivative represents the rate at which a function's value changes with respect to a change in its input.  

A4a) Newton's first law of motion states that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force.  

A5a) Electric current is the flow of electric charge, typically measured in amperes (A).  

A6a) Gravitational potential energy is the energy an object possesses due to its position in a gravitational field, usually relative to the Earth.  

A7a) According to the Brønsted-Lowry definition, an acid is a substance that donates a proton (H⁺) to another substance.  

A8a) A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed in the process.  

A9a) Oxidation is the loss of electrons by a substance during a chemical reaction.  

A10a) Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water into glucose and oxygen.  

A11a) Natural selection is the process by which organisms better adapted to their environment tend to survive and reproduce, passing on advantageous traits.  

A12a) DNA replication is the process by which a cell makes an exact copy of its DNA before cell division.  

A13a) A computer algorithm is a step-by-step procedure or set of rules for solving a problem or performing a computation.  

A14a) Object-oriented programming is a programming paradigm based on the concept of "objects," which contain data and methods to manipulate that data.  

A15a) A database is an organized collection of data that can be accessed, managed, and updated efficiently.  

A16a) Feudalism is a historical social system in which land was held by lords in exchange for service and labor from vassals or serfs.  

A17a) The Renaissance was a period of cultural, artistic, and intellectual revival in Europe, roughly from the 14th to the 17th century, emphasizing humanism and learning.  

A18a) The Industrial Revolution was a period of major technological, economic, and social change in the 18th and 19th centuries, marked by the shift from agrarian to industrial societies.  

A19a) Utilitarianism is a philosophical theory that holds that the best action is the one that maximizes overall happiness or utility.  

A20a) Epistemology is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature, scope, and sources of knowledge.  

A21a) Existentialism is a philosophical movement that emphasizes individual freedom, choice, and responsibility in creating meaning in an otherwise indifferent or meaningless world.  

A22a) Supply and demand is an economic model describing how the quantity of a good supplied and the quantity demanded interact to determine price.  

A23a) Inflation is the rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, reducing purchasing power.  

A24a) Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative foregone when making a choice.  

A25a) A metaphor is a figure of speech in which one thing is described as if it were another to suggest a similarity.  

A26a) Syntax is the set of rules that govern the structure and arrangement of words and phrases in a language.  

A27a) Narrative point of view refers to the perspective from which a story is told, such as first-person, third-person, or omniscient.  

A28a) Informed consent is the ethical practice of ensuring that a person fully understands and agrees to a procedure or action before it is carried out.  

A29a) Beneficence is the ethical principle that obligates individuals to act in ways that promote the well-being of others.  

A30a) Moral relativism is the view that moral judgments and ethical standards are not absolute but are shaped by cultural, societal, or personal circumstances.
