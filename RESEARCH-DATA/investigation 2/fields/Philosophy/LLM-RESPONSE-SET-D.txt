A7d) Utilitarianism is the ethical theory that actions are right if they maximize overall happiness; its principal tenet is the greatest happiness principle.  
A8d) Epistemology is the study of knowledge, addressing what knowledge is, how it is acquired, and what justifies belief.  
A9d) Deontology emphasizes duties and moral rules, in contrast to consequentialism, which evaluates actions solely by their outcomes.  
A10d) The social contract is the idea that individuals consent to give up certain freedoms in exchange for social order and protection.  
A11d) Phenomenology studies conscious experience from the first-person perspective, focusing on structures and intentionality of experience.  
A12d) Existentialism focuses on human freedom, responsibility, and the search for meaning in an indifferent or absurd world.  

A13d) Kant’s categorical imperative can be applied by evaluating whether an action’s maxim is universalizable and respects individuals as ends in themselves.  
A14d) Aristotle’s virtue ethics emphasizes character formation through habituation, while contemporary approaches incorporate social and psychological insights.  
A15d) Determinism raises questions about moral responsibility, accountability, and the extent of individual freedom.  
A16d) Rawls’ justice theory recommends policies that improve the welfare of the least advantaged while ensuring fair equality of opportunity.  
A17d) Interpretations of Nietzsche’s will to power influence political thought by emphasizing self-overcoming, hierarchy, or personal excellence.  
A18d) Mind-body dualism posits separation of mind and body, whereas neuroscience demonstrates interactions that challenge strict dualism.

A20d) Nietzsche’s Übermensch can guide leadership by promoting self-mastery, creativity, and transformative influence beyond conventional authority.

A22d) Wittgenstein argues that language shapes reality by structuring how concepts and experiences can be expressed and understood.  
A23d) Confucian virtues, such as ren and li, guide ethical behavior, family relations, and governance in contemporary East Asian societies.  
A24d) Existentialist literature portrays the struggle for authenticity by depicting characters confronting freedom, responsibility, and meaning.  

A25d) Utilitarianism can inform healthcare policy by prioritizing actions that maximize overall well-being, while balancing fairness and individual rights.

A28d) Kantian ethics evaluates AI by emphasizing duty, respect for human autonomy, and avoiding treating humans merely as means to an end.  
A29d) Education should prioritize critical thinking over memorization to foster reasoning, ethical judgment, and independent thought.  
A30d) Free will can be compatible with legal responsibility by recognizing constrained autonomy and social accountability within deterministic frameworks.
